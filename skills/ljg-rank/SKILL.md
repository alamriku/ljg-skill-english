# Rank-reduction engine

Give it a field; it gives you the rank.

## What rank is

Rank is not "key factors", not "core principles", not "main takeaways".

Rank is this: how many genuinely independent generators does this field have? And with those few, can every phenomenon be grown back, one by one? Only if it can have you found it.

Growing them back is only the floor. **The yardstick David Deutsch sets in *The Beginning of Infinity*** is two gates a good explanation must pass:

- **Reach** - it implies not only the phenomena on your list but also phenomena off it, and reality confirms them.
- **Hard to vary** - every generator and every detail is forced by the phenomena. Change one thing and the prediction collapses.

A bad explanation can be told ten different ways - it is not explaining anything, it is spreading wide by staying vague. A good explanation has only one telling that fits all the phenomena; move one piece and it falls down.

Finding rank means finding that kind of explanation - the kind that collapses if you move one piece.

## How to find it: look up first, then dig down

Before digging, look up once and see what the field is standing on - **which few basic assumptions hold it?**

### 0. The basic assumptions (look up, see the ceiling)

Any body of theory, on the old Aristotelian rule, needs a few self-evident starting points. A religion may stand on three: that its scripture is true and all thinking starts there; that its God exists and is one; that people are made and loved by that God. Those three are not open to "why" - they are held by belief, not by proof. Shake any one and the whole doctrine falls.

Every field has something like that. In physics it is "the laws of nature are stable across space and time" - you cannot prove it experimentally, since experiment already presupposes it. In economics it is "people choose by ordering their own preferences" - not an observed conclusion but the ticket to entry. In analytic philosophy it is "language can refer to the world" - the entire house is built on that ground.

Before reducing rank, dig those few out and **write them down plainly**.

Two working questions to put to the field:

- **What may not be asked here?** (ask and you are treated as an outsider, or as giving offence)
- **What holds only by being believed?** (without it, every argument downstream loses its pivot)

What comes back is this field's set of basic assumptions.

**Assumption and rank, in one line**:

- **The basic assumptions are the ceiling** - nothing above them, held by belief.
- **The root rank is the floor** - chase it down layer by layer until you cannot chase further, found by digging.

Two directions, one up, one down. **Without the floor you go in circles; without seeing the ceiling you end up stating an article of faith as a truth** - the second is more dangerous, because it also sounds like truth.

How it enters the piece: a sentence or two at the start, stating the basic assumptions plainly. No argument for them, no explaining "why these ones" - their position is to be unproven. Then start digging the rank.

### 1-7. Digging down: the penetration process

Criteria can only be checked afterwards; all the effort of finding rank goes into the process. However strict the criteria, they can only weed out false roots - and only if the process actually went to the bottom.

Seven steps, run in your head, never written into the piece:

1. **Lay out the phenomena** - 10+ representative phenomena from the field, laid out, not explained. The point is to leave the rank a target it has to regenerate.
2. **List the candidates** - for each phenomenon ask "why is it like this" and post every candidate generator. This step is necessarily messy, with false roots mixed in.
3. **Recursive descent** - the heart of penetration, and the easiest step to get wrong. The wrong way is trying to dig to the bottom in one stroke: guessing "what is most fundamental" straight from the phenomena. Standing on the phenomenon layer you cannot see the foundation, only the layer under your feet, and nine times out of ten the bottom you guess is floating.

   The right way is to sink one layer at a time. **First find only the first-layer generators - the layer immediately beneath the phenomena, visible and within reach. Collect them, treat that layer as new ground, and stand on it.** Once steady, ask again: "is there anything more fundamental beneath this layer?" Only now does the second layer show itself - from the phenomenon layer the question could not even be posed. Collect the second layer, make it new ground, stand on it, look down again. Recurse down level by level until you stand somewhere and nothing more fundamental can be asked - that layer is the root rank.

   This is the patient work of opening a black box one layer at a time: the current layer is everything you can observe, and only once it is clear and you are standing steady does the next one open. Skipping levels is greed for speed; skip one layer and the root rank is one layer hollower.

   For example, reducing "starting a company": the first stroke turns up vision, execution, team, market - all plausible, but really just surface "key factors", and stopping there means you never sank. Treat those four as new ground, stand on them, and ask: where does vision come from? what holds execution up? Only now are you on the second layer. Stand down layer by layer until you cannot, and you have touched the root rank.
4. **Merge the same source** - two candidates may be one generator showing two faces; merge them. The test in one line: remove one - would the other still appear on its own?
5. **Cut** - take one away: can the rest regenerate every phenomenon? What can be cut goes, what cannot stays. This step lands the criterion of minimality.
6. **Regenerate** - with what remains, walk the phenomenon list from the top and see whether each one can be reproduced. The one that cannot points at a missing generator.
7. **Prediction + variation, both gates** - pass them together:
   - **Predict off the list** (reach): can these few imply phenomena not on the list? And are those real?
   - **Variation test** (hard to vary): pick any one and change a detail - a condition, a direction, a magnitude. Is the prediction still right? If it is, that root is loose and false, and can be swapped out; if changing one thing collapses it, that root is tight and real, and cannot be moved. In Deutsch's words: whatever you can change is not part of its core.

Only after the seven steps are the candidates fit to be put on the criteria table.

Run the process thoroughly in your head, but **keep it out of the piece** - like the criteria, it is internal craft, not a narrative hook. The reader never sees the digging, but they can feel that it went to rock.

## Five internal criteria

Once the process is done, these five are the gates you run afterwards - **also not written for the reader**.

**The first three govern the skeleton (is the shape of these roots right)**:
1. **Generativity** - these few can regenerate every phenomenon
2. **Minimality** - remove any one and some phenomenon no longer follows (this governs *number* - none may be dropped)
3. **Independence** - for any two, you can find a case where one moved and the other did not

**The last two govern explanatory power (good enough to count as a good explanation) - from Deutsch's *The Beginning of Infinity***:
4. **Reach** - it implies phenomena off the list, and those are real
5. **Hard to vary** - change any detail of any root and the prediction goes wrong (this governs *detail* - nothing may be moved)

The fiercest is the fifth. Minimality governs number - nothing may be taken away; hard-to-vary governs detail - nothing may be moved. If a generator "also works said another way" or "holds with a small adjustment", it is a loose false root whose predictive power is faked - achieved by spreading wide through vagueness, not by precision. **A bad explanation survives any change; a good one collapses at the first touch** - that is the core distinction Deutsch draws.

Fail one and start over - go back to step 3 of the process and keep chasing. Failing the fifth usually means not that a root is missing but that one is written too loosely - force yourself to rewrite it until it can only be said this way.

Walk the criteria firmly in your head, but **keep them off the page** - no visible verification moves like "let us cut one and see" or "let us pull a root out and test". Those break the reader's single breath from chaos to simplicity. The reader never sees the verification but can feel that what is underneath is hard.

## Two levels: find the root rank, then see whether a coordinate system can sit on it

Once the criteria are passed, the few lines surface. Do not start writing yet - look at their shape on two levels.

**Level one: find the root rank (the skeleton)**

The root rank is the field's real skeleton and the end point of the recursive descent - the layer you reach when you can stand no lower. Do not presuppose how many roots there are or how they relate; just find them. The shape may be:

- a few standing side by side (pillars)
- a few nested one inside another (the deepest one a meta-proposition)
- a few biting and pushing each other (a loop)
- a few following one another (a chain)

Having read the root rank, the reader holds a **worldview** - the field's skeleton, a new way of seeing.

**Shape interrogation - the anti-collapse gate** (once the lines are found, mandatory self-check before writing)

Nine shapes, run through every one:

- A drill section? (layer resting on layer, the deepest one a meta-proposition)
- Side by side? (pillars, orthogonal and slidable)
- A feedback loop? (pushing each other, plus and minus)
- A stage chain? (one segment after another, in time or logic)
- A tension spectrum? (one line pulled from both ends, cases landing in between)
- A branching tree? (one splits into several, which split again)
- A network? (many-to-many, no hierarchy)
- An oscillation? (rising and falling, alternating rhythm)
- Multiple slices? (classification across more than two axes)

**An AI reaches for "drill section" first - that is the lazy anchor.** Any concept can be split into three layers (surface / middle / bottom), and it looks tidy, but many things are not vertically structured at all. Note: the depth won by recursive descent is not the same as these lazy three layers - descent is earned, you really stood on each layer and checked that it generates the one above, and only then does a vertical structure count; laying out three layers without descending is the lazy version. Also, how many vertical layers there are and what shape the roots *within* a layer take (side by side / in tension / pushing each other) are two separate questions.

Mandatory interrogation:
- Do these roots really have an order or a nesting? (drill vs side by side)
- Or are they actually pushing each other? (drill vs feedback loop)
- Or is there really a temporal or logical order? (drill vs stage chain)
- Or is it really just one spectrum? (drill vs spectrum)

Only after ruling out the other eight may you land on "drill section". **This elimination cannot be skipped** - the cost of skipping is a distorted root rank that crushes sequence, feedback and tension all into nesting.

**Level two: can an operating coordinate system sit on top?**

Once the root rank is found, ask one more question: **is there a line in it** that unfolds into several independently adjustable state parameters?

Example: the third root of one root rank is "rocket geometry vs pendulum geometry". Rocket geometry needs two points - the vision and current reality; treat the clarity of each as an adjustable axis and a two-dimensional coordinate system grows. The coordinate system is not a replacement for the root rank; it is a child grown on one of its lines.

If it unfolds, the coordinate system **sits on top of** the root rank. Having read it, the reader gets one more thing: an **instrument** - drop any concrete situation into the frame and see which quadrant and which layer it lands in.

The two levels do not fight - the mechanism says "what it is", the coordinate system says "where it is". Two different questions, and both can be given.

**When to stack it and when not to**:

- The root rank contains a line that inherently needs two or more orthogonal, continuously adjustable state parameters -> stack it
- Every root is a one-off action or a stage (nothing slides) -> do not force it
- Forcing a mechanism into a coordinate system distorts it - e.g. drawing "ignition / condensation" as two axes and placing each phenomenon at an (x, y) point is meaningless, because they are not continuous quantities coexisting at one moment

When a coordinate system can be stacked, the rank goes up a grade - the reader walks away with both a worldview and an instrument.

**Frame interrogation - the anti-collapse gate** (mandatory self-check before unfolding level two)

Before committing to the stack, ask: is the line I want to unfold really *two independent slidable axes*?

- Could it actually be a single spectrum? (then use a slider - a case lands at a point, which fits better than four quadrants)
- Could it actually be a feedback relation? (then draw the loop, marked + / -)
- Could it actually be stages? (then use a chain or steps, marked with the transition conditions)
- Could it actually branch? (then use a tree)

**The AI's other lazy anchor is the 2x2 matrix.** Consulting and strategy training data is full of them, any two variables can be cut into four quadrants, and it looks like analysis. But many root ranks are not two independent dimensions - they are a single spectrum, a feedback loop, a chain.

Mandatory interrogation:
- Are the two dimensions really independent? (independent vs two faces of one line)
- Do they really slide? (continuously adjustable vs discrete stages)
- Does every one of the four quadrants correspond to something real? (three empty boxes means a 2x2 was never right)

Only after ruling out spectrum / loop / chain / tree may you confirm the 2x2.

## Natural English - the anti-collapse gate

Before writing, say it three times: **"would a person who has never read a consulting deck say it this way?"**

Say it again after each paragraph. If the answer is no, *do not swap words* - **knock the paragraph down and think it again.**

The stiff constructions easiest to fall into (when you hit one, rewrite the paragraph, do not pick at words):

| Stiff | Natural |
|---|---|
| It is predicated upon the previous one | The previous one holds it up |
| It possesses an intrinsic directionality | It moves that way on its own |
| This distinction addresses X rather than Y | This asks about X, not Y |
| This means that... | In other words... / That is why... |
| In the process of ... | While ... |
| With respect to ... | For ... |
| It is X that does Y (cleft sentences) | Say it straight |
| Perform an analysis of | Analyze |
| Abstract nominalizations ("directionality", "operationalization") | A concrete verb or object |

**What needs changing is not the word but the frame you express from** - move out of consulting-deck syntax into plain, concrete writing with a human voice.

Scanning for words afterwards only fixes the surface. The root cause is thinking in that register in the first place. When rewriting, put the stiff version aside and think again, in plain language, about how this should be said.

Read it aloud when finished and listen for where the ear snags. Where it snags, **rewrite the paragraph**, do not patch it.

## How to write it

One continuous piece of prose.

Take the reader on a walk - from "this field looks like a mess" to "so it is these few lines pulling everything". No chapters, no subheadings; let the reasoning push itself forward.

Three requirements:
- **Read in one sitting** - even someone unfamiliar with the field cannot stop
- **Memorable** - afterwards they can turn around and explain it to a friend in one line
- **A drop** - the contrast from chaos to extreme simplicity is where the beauty of rank reduction lives

**Two halves (if a coordinate system is stacked)**:

- First half: work the root rank's skeleton through - the meta-proposition, the dynamics, the shape. The reader finishes it holding a worldview.
- Second half: unfold the coordinate system on one of the root rank's lines - the two axes, the four quadrants, what each box corresponds to. The reader finishes it holding an instrument.

Do not cut hard between the halves. Let the reasoning carry over - the last line of the first half should ideally leave a hook, something like "so what does this mechanism look like in a concrete situation?", and lead the reader down.

## Close with one ASCII structure diagram - the form follows the skeleton

Saying "these few lines" is not enough - **draw the relation between them**.

And there is more than one way to draw. However the root rank is shaped, the frame should be shaped the same way. Before drawing, ask: **what shape are this rank's relations, in themselves?** Then pick the form. **Form follows skeleton, never the reverse.**

Nine frame families - this table is the index from skeleton to drawing:

| Root rank shape | Nature of the relation | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| Several side by side, independent, slidable | orthogonal | two-axis / multi-axis coordinate system |
| Layer resting on layer (the deepest a meta-proposition) | nested penetration | drill section |
| One line pulled from both ends | tension / opposition | spectrum / slider |
| Pushing each other, positively and negatively | feedback loop | loop diagram (marked + / -) |
| One segment after another | stage progression | chain / steps |
| One splits into several, which split again | hierarchical branching | tree |
| Many-to-many interlocking | coupled network | network diagram |
| Rising and falling, alternating rhythm | oscillation | waveform / oscillation curve |
| Multi-dimensional classification (e.g. abstraction x distance x time) | multi-dimensional | N-axis / multiple slices |

**Hard rule: pure ASCII characters only.** No Unicode symbols of any kind (no arrows, no box-drawing characters, no bullet dots, no heavy triangles).

Allowed character set: letters, digits, spaces, and `- = | + * / \ < > ^ v [ ] ( ) { } . , : ; _ #`.

Substitution table (banned on the left / replacement on the right):

- box-drawing corners and tees -> `+`
- horizontal rules -> `-` (or `=` for a heavy line)
- vertical rules -> `|`
- right arrows -> `->`
- left arrows -> `<-`
- up arrows -> `^` or `|^`
- down arrows -> `v` or `|v`
- double-headed arrows -> `<->` or `<--->`
- bullet dots -> `*` or `o`
- multiplication sign -> `x`

Nine examples follow - each with a minimal usable ASCII diagram and one line of **requirements**. Which one you pick follows the root rank shape the process uncovered.

### 1. Two-axis / multi-axis coordinate system

For: the root rank's lines are orthogonal, independent, slidable.

**Requirements**: the diagram must mark the coordinates of a few concrete phenomena - where this one lands, where that one lands. Seeing how the frame is used at a glance is how the reader learns it. Axes with no demonstration points are an empty display.

**Coordinate systems come in two grades, which decide what goes inside each quadrant**:

- *Attribute coordinate systems* - the two axes are categories or state attributes (light-heavy x emotional-rational, extravert-introvert x intuition-sensing). Each quadrant is only a label; no dynamics happen inside the box. Follow the requirement above - mark demonstration coordinates and examples at the corners, and draw nothing inside the boxes.

- *Dynamical coordinate systems* - the two axes are dynamical dimensions, and the system in each quadrant evolves its own behaviour over time. Potential functions (conservative-dissipative x single-multiple minima), phase transitions (temperature-pressure), games (zero-sum vs non-zero-sum x one-shot vs repeated). Flat labels waste such a system - each quadrant should be upgraded to a **miniature ASCII picture inside a rectangular frame**, drawing directly how the system moves in that box.

The test in one line: **does the system in this box have a movement you could draw?** Yes - upgrade to a dynamical picture; no - fall back to an attribute label.

**How to draw the dynamical pictures**:
- Each quadrant is a rectangle (bordered with `+ - |`, like a small framed ASCII picture)
- At the top inside the frame, a miniature of that box's dynamics - terrain / state / arrows / a ball / a curve, your choice, pure ASCII
- In the middle, one line describing what the system is doing in this box
- At the bottom, the quadrant label plus one or two typical examples
- The four rectangles are separated by the axes (the axis lines run between them)
- Pixel-perfect alignment is not required, but the structure inside each frame must be consistent

Potential-function example:

```
                                single minimum
                                      |^
                                      |
                                      |
   +--------------------------+       |       +--------------------------+
   |                          |       |       |                          |
   |        \________/        |       |       |        \________/        |
   |           o <->          |       |       |           o .            |
   |                          |       |       |                          |
   | one valley, ball swings  |       |       | one valley, ball slows   |
   | forever                  |       |       | and settles              |
   |                          |       |       |                          |
   | conservative + single    |       |       | dissipative + single     |
   | [pendulum / oscillator]  |       |       | [convex optimization]    |
   +--------------------------+       |       +--------------------------+
                                      |
   conservative <-----------------+-------------------------> dissipative
   (energy conserved)                 |   (energy drained by the environment)
                                      |
   +--------------------------+       |       +--------------------------+
   |                          |       |       |                          |
   |   \_/\_/\_/\_/\_/        |       |       |   \_/\_/\_/\_/\_/        |
   |   <-o   o   o->          |       |       |     o   .   o            |
   |                          |       |       |                          |
   | many valleys, energy     |       |       | many valleys, each ball  |
   | crosses the ridges       |       |       | settles and stays        |
   |                          |       |       |                          |
   | conservative + multiple  |       |       | dissipative + multiple   |

```

At a glance the reader sees terrain plus the ball's fate framed as a picture in each box, so how the system evolves is directly visible. A label cannot do that.

### 2. Drill section

For: the root rank is layer resting on layer, the deepest a meta-proposition. The reader penetrates downward.

```
+------------------------------------+
| surface  phenomenon                |
| [users open the app every day]     |
+------------------------------------+
                 |
                 v
+------------------------------------+
| middle   mechanism                 |
| [notifications + feedback + habit] |
+------------------------------------+
                 |
                 v
+------------------------------------+
| bottom   meta-proposition          |
| [intermittent dopamine reward]     |
+------------------------------------+
```

**Requirements**: mark a concrete example at every layer, so the reader sees the path from phenomenon -> mechanism -> meta-proposition. The deepest layer is the meta-proposition; do not leave it blank - a blank means you never drilled to the bottom.

### 3. Spectrum / slider

For: the root rank is one line pulled from both ends, with cases landing somewhere between.

```
extreme A <===============o===============> extreme B
[features near A]         ^           [features near B]
                          |
                     case X lands here
```

**Requirements**: label the features at both ends and place at least one concrete case somewhere in between. Two ends with no case = an empty display; the reader has to see how the ruler measures.

### 4. Feedback loop

For: the root rank is a loop of roots pushing each other; the signs must be drawn.

```
        A --(+)--> B
        ^          |
        |          |
       (-)        (+)
        |          |
        |          v
        D <--(+)-- C
```

**Requirements**: mark every arrow `+` or `-`, and note beside the diagram the overall effect of each loop, reinforcing or balancing. A loop diagram without signs is not a diagram.

### 5. Chain / steps

For: the root rank is stage progression, one segment after another in time or logic.

Chain, laid out horizontally:

```
A ---> B ---> C ---> D
[stage] [stage] [stage] [stage]
```

Steps, laid out vertically (rising):

```
                 +----+
                 | D  |
            +----+
            | C  |
       +----+
       | B  |
  +----+
  | A  |
  +----+
```

**Requirements**: name each segment and mark the transition conditions (what pushes A to B, what lifts C to D). A chain without transition conditions is a list, not a mechanism.

### 6. Tree

For: the root rank splits into several, which split again.

```
              root rank
              /   |   \
             A    B    C
            / \   |   /|\
           a   b  d  x y z
```

**Requirements**: name the classification principle at each branch point (by what is the layer above cut into the layer below) - an unnamed principle makes the tree mush.

### 7. Network

For: the root rank is many-to-many with no hierarchy.

```
   A --------- B
   |\         /|
   | \       / |
   |  \     /  |
   |   \   /   |
   |    \ /    |
   |     X     |
   |    / \    |
   |   /   \   |
   |  /     \  |
   | /       \ |
   D --------- C
```

**Requirements**: give each edge a direction (where there is one) and a strength (heavy `=` / light `-`), and mark the key nodes. A mat of undifferentiated lines is a network but not a skeleton.

### 8. Waveform / oscillation curve

For: the root rank rises and falls in alternating rhythm (seasons, cycles, boom and bust, focus and drift).

```
   /\          /\          /\
  /  \        /  \        /  \
 /    \      /    \      /    \
/      \____/      \____/      \____
^                    ^
trough               peak
[behaviour at the    [behaviour at the
 trough]              peak]
```

**Requirements**: mark at least one trough-peak pair with concrete examples, so the reader sees the states at different points of the cycle - an unlabelled waveform is a decorative line.

### 9. N-axis / multiple slices

For: classification across more than two axes (say abstraction x distance x time). 3D is hard to draw clearly, so draw the pairwise 2D slices:

```
slice 1: abstraction x distance     slice 2: abstraction x time
       high abstraction                   high abstraction
         ^                                   ^
         |                                   |
         |                                   |
near <---+---> far                  past <---+---> present
         |                                   |
         |                                   |
         v                                   v
       low abstraction                    low abstraction
```

**Requirements**: mark at least one case per slice and which quadrant it falls in; the set is complete only when every pair is drawn. A missing pair leaves a blind spot.

---

**Stacked (root rank + frame) means two diagrams**: the upper one is the root rank's skeleton (one of the nine above), the lower one the frame stacked on one of its lines (with demonstration coordinates / points / paths marked). Tie them with a line - something like "unfolds here ->" or "zoom into this box" - telling the reader which box of the first diagram the second is nested in. One look and they see the skeleton and the instrument hidden inside one of its boxes, worldview and operating instrument together.

No rigid template. Show the bones - name the generators, the direction of the relations, the sign of the effects. One look at the diagram and the skeleton sticks.
